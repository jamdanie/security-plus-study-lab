# Give a visual study session a clear goal

[Game comparison](steam-data-center-games.md) · [Mission directory](hands-on-missions.md)

Choose one challenge before opening a game. The activities below are original study prompts; they do not claim the game simulates permissions, backups, or data labels. Use the offline exercise whenever the product lacks the needed feature.

| Challenge | What to do | What to explain afterward |
|---|---|---|
| Draw a dependency | Map a supported service and its path | Which shared component can still stop it? |
| Review an access request | Use [temporary grants](../labs/31-temporary-access/README.md) | Scope, expiry, and approval |
| Protect a recovery copy | Use [backup exposure](../labs/32-backup-exposure/README.md) | Reachability versus restoration evidence |
| Choose a sharing destination | Use [data handling](../labs/33-data-handling/README.md) | Why the destination meets or violates policy |

## Try a changed condition

After your first answer, change one fact: the grant expires, the backup is reconnected, or the destination becomes public. Explain whether the answer changes and why.

For a supported in-game action, record the actual observation before drawing a real-world conclusion. A game score can reflect its scoring rules without proving that a security requirement is met.

## Keep a small result

Save a diagram or decision table with your prediction, observed or supplied evidence, explanation, and limit. Mark an offline policy decision as an offline exercise. Mark gameplay as gameplay.

Use [questions 19](../practice-tests/permissions-and-data-19.md) and [three cards](../anki/permissions-and-data.txt) afterward. [Learning notes](../study/hands-on-notes.md) shows how to describe results without claiming work you have not done.
