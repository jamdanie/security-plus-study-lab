## 1. A fictional incident uses spoofed victim addresses to trigger larger replies from several intermediary servers. Which pair describes reply direction and size increase?

- A. Reflection and amplification.
- B. Encryption and hashing.
- C. Authorization and federation.
- D. Backup and replication.

## 2. One sample request is 60 bytes and its response is 600 bytes using the same measurement convention. What is the ratio?

- A. 0.1.
- B. 10.
- C. 600.
- D. The exact outage duration.

## 3. A service is slow during a popular event. What does that alone prove?

- A. A botnet exists.
- B. Reflection occurred.
- C. Availability is affected, but the cause needs investigation.
- D. Every request is malicious.

## 4. Visitor policy requires an escort. A badged visitor has no escort and asks an employee to hold the door. What best meets policy?

- A. Admit because the badge is enough.
- B. Assume the visitor is an attacker.
- C. Disable the entry control.
- D. Verify and arrange required sponsorship and escort before admission.

## 5. A camera records an entrance. Does recording alone physically prevent unauthorized entry?

- A. No; observation and entry prevention are different functions.
- B. Yes; all cameras lock doors.
- C. Yes; recording verifies every identity.
- D. Only if the video filename is changed.

## 6. A tabletop identifies an outdated escalation list. What is the best follow-up?

- A. Claim live recovery was tested.
- B. Assign correction and verify the revised escalation route.
- C. Ignore it because it is a discussion.
- D. Delete the incident record.

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. **A.** Reflection explains replies redirected to the victim; amplification explains larger responses. B and C describe unrelated controls. D concerns data availability mechanisms, not the given traffic.

2. **B.** 600 divided by 60 is 10. A reverses the division. C uses a size without dividing. D is a different measurement.

3. **C.** C stays within the evidence. A, B, and D require information not supplied.

4. **D.** D follows the stated conditions. A ignores the escort requirement. B invents motive. C weakens the control without justification.

5. **A.** A distinguishes the functions. B and C assume capabilities not supplied. D is unrelated.

6. **B.** B turns the finding into a checkable improvement. A claims work not performed. C discards the finding. D removes useful evidence.

</details>
