# Governance: which document answers the question?

Governance establishes direction, responsibility, and oversight. Start by asking whether the scenario needs a broad rule, a required detail, instructions, or optional advice.

## Compare the documents

This is a common study distinction. Organizations may use different document names; read their definitions and the scenario's stated requirements.

| Document | Purpose | Fictional example |
|---|---|---|
| Policy | Establishes organizational direction and expectations | Sensitive records must be restricted to approved users |
| Standard | Defines mandatory details supporting a policy | Remote administrator access must use the approved authentication configuration |
| Procedure | Gives steps for carrying out a task | Validate approval, create the account, assign the role, verify access, record completion |
| Guideline | Offers recommended approaches | Suggested ways to organize a team's access-review meeting |

The length of a document does not determine its type. A short procedure is still a procedure if it gives the steps.

## Worked decision

A help-desk team knows that departed employees must lose access. Technicians need a consistent sequence for disabling accounts and handling active sessions.

The missing item is a procedure. A policy can establish the obligation, but repeating that obligation does not explain how to perform the task. A guideline would offer advice rather than the required workflow in this example.

Change the question to "Which organization-wide rule authorizes this expectation?" and a policy becomes the closer match.

## Responsibility and review

An information owner has authority and responsibility for specified information and its handling requirements. A technical team may implement the approved access decisions. The exact approval and implementation roles must be assigned by the organization; administrator access alone does not establish authority to approve every business decision.

Keep documents usable by identifying scope, owner, approval, effective version, and review conditions. When a process changes, review the supporting instructions as well as the broad rule.
