# Exercise 15: is the laptop ready to leave?

Status: ready to try. Offline fictional exercise; do not erase any real device.

A business intends to donate a laptop. The record shows:

| Field | Recorded evidence |
|---|---|
| Asset tag | LAP-027 |
| Serial number | Not recorded |
| Assigned user | Former employee |
| Accountable owner | Not assigned |
| Data | May include customer files |
| Retention review | Not completed |
| Proposed action | Delete the visible files and donate |
| Storage media | Not verified |

## Your decision

Explain what must be resolved before authorizing release. Do not choose a destructive method from incomplete media information.

<details>
<summary>Suggested analysis</summary>

Confirm the physical device and a reliable identifier. Assign or identify the accountable owner and reconcile custody. Determine data and retention requirements before removal. Identify the storage media, then use an approved sanitization process appropriate to sensitivity and destination.

The proposed file deletion does not establish sanitization. Document the operation's outcome, its review, and final transfer. Do not assume an old user assignment establishes current business approval.

</details>

## Change the evidence

A technician reports that an approved tool completed successfully, but the result record lists a different asset tag. Is that enough to release LAP-027?

<details>
<summary>Explanation</summary>

No. Reconcile the identifier mismatch before attributing the result to this device. A successful operation on the wrong or unidentified asset does not establish the target was sanitized.

</details>

Explain how you would verify the record without repeating a destructive operation unnecessarily.
