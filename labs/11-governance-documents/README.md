# Exercise 11: choose the missing document

Status: ready to try. Fictional offline exercise.

A small business already has a policy requiring prompt removal of access when an employee leaves. Technicians handle departures differently, and managers cannot determine whether all assigned systems were checked.

## Decide

1. Which document should provide the operational sequence?
2. What should record completion and exceptions?
3. Does a technician's ability to disable accounts automatically authorize them to change the company's retention policy?
4. How would you check that the new instructions work?

<details>
<summary>Suggested analysis</summary>

A procedure should specify the assigned actions, required approvals, systems to check, and verification. A ticket or other approved record can capture actions, times, responsible staff, and unresolved exceptions.

Technical capability does not by itself authorize policy changes. Refer retention questions to the assigned authority.

Test the procedure with an authorized fictional or test account. Check both removal of access and preservation required by the organization's instructions. Review omissions and update the approved procedure.

</details>

## Classify the next request

Management now wants required settings for remote administrator authentication, not the sequence for creating an account. Which document type fits?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A standard fits mandatory technical details under the study distinctions used here. A procedure describes how to apply and verify those details. The actual organization must define its document hierarchy.
